title: Smallholder digitization — what actually works
date: 2026-04-10
excerpt: Most farmer-registry projects die after the pilot. The ones that survive have one thing in common, and it's not the technology.
ctaTopic: smallholder digitization
---
Governments and cooperatives have gotten good at funding farmer-registry pilots. What they're less good at is getting those pilots to survive past the pilot phase. I've now seen enough of these projects, on both sides, to notice the pattern in the ones that actually stick.

The projects that survive give the farmer something back immediately — a record they can show a bank for a loan, a document a buyer will actually accept, proof of yield history that gets them into a premium market. The ones that fail collect data for a dashboard some ministry official checks quarterly, and ask farmers to participate for nothing in return. Farmers are rational. If the registry doesn't do anything for them, participation drops the moment the enumerators leave.

That's the whole insight, really: digitization has to be farmer-first before it's useful to anyone else — the government, the cooperative, or the buyer sitting downstream of the data.
