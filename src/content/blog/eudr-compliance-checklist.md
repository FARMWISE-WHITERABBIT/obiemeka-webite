Every exporter I talk to has heard of the EU Deforestation Regulation. Fewer than one in five can tell me, specifically, what their business needs to produce to clear it. That gap is where shipments get held at port, and it's almost always avoidable with six months of lead time.

## What EUDR actually asks for

The regulation isn't really about deforestation monitoring in the abstract — it's about **traceability to a geolocated plot** and a **documented due-diligence statement** that a buyer can attach to their own filing. Two things, done consistently, cover most of the requirement:

1. GPS polygon or point data for every plot the produce came from
2. A due-diligence statement showing the produce wasn't grown on land deforested after December 2020

Everything else — supplier declarations, chain-of-custody documents, risk assessments — exists to support those two things.

## Where exporters actually get stuck

It's rarely the compliance paperwork itself. It's upstream: cooperatives and aggregators who don't have plot-level GPS data for their smallholder network, because nobody asked for it before EUDR made it mandatory. Retrofitting that data collection under deadline pressure is expensive and slow. Building it into your sourcing relationships now is not.

## A 90-day starting point

If you're an exporter shipping to the EU and you don't yet have plot-level data on your supply base, the sequence that actually works is: map your highest-volume suppliers first, get their plots geolocated, build the due-diligence statement template once, then replicate it down the supplier list. Trying to do all suppliers simultaneously is how these projects stall.

None of this is exotic. It's operational discipline applied early enough that it doesn't become a shipment-blocking emergency.
