# Research notes: plantain's fuller commercial use in Nigeria

Reviewed: 5 November 2026. Supports `src/content/blog/plantain-full-potential-nigeria.md` and future video preparation.

## Editorial position

Present fuller utilization as Obi's argument, not a measured national utilization rate. The evidence supports established uses, working hygiene businesses abroad, and further development opportunities, but not a universal ranking above cassava, maize, or oil palm. No current Nigerian market-size, revenue, profit-per-hectare, or commercial-adoption estimate was established.

The article distinguishes plantain from related banana research, and experimental material performance from a qualified finished product. “Stalk” is explained as the pseudostem; it is not the fruit-bunch peduncle or a woody trunk.

## Commercial evidence added after the user supplied examples

- [Saathi impact page](https://saathipads.com/pages/our-impact): first-party product and operating-model evidence. Environmental and social claims are identified as company descriptions, not independently audited findings. Avoid repeating “chemical-free” or medical superiority claims.
- [SHE FAQ](https://sheinnovates.com/faq/): first-party production and sales model. The complete product is not represented as fully biodegradable merely because its core is.
- [UNICEF StartUp Lab profile of Kodu Technology](https://www.unicef.org/ghana/blog/sustainable-solution-period-poverty): programme participant using banana and plantain residues, with a market-ready product described in May 2025. No Nigerian factory or certification was verified.
- [Business Insider's filmed Saathi report](https://www.businessinsider.com/how-banana-plant-waste-is-turned-into-biodegradable-menstrual-pads-2022-3): linked for visual context; not reproduced or embedded as Obi's own video.
- User's Rwanda link: [GLF community account](https://thinklandscape.globallandscapesforum.org/97881/rwanda-banana-fiber-menstrual-pads/), a separate reusable banana/cotton initiative. Do not conflate its construction or disposal claims with SHE or Saathi.
- User's Punch link: a 2017 report about Saathi in India, not evidence of a Nigerian factory. No old skin-safety or “chemical-free” assertions were repeated.
- “Up to 3,000 pads per stem” is not used as a production assumption. It depends on the feedstock, pad construction, fibre loading, recovery and rejected material, and is not a verified Nigerian plantain yield.
- Coloured-water demonstrations and UV exposure in a video are not presented as a complete product qualification or guaranteed sterilization process.

## Questions to resolve before filming a commercial demonstration

- Identify the actual local cultivar and keep it consistent through trials.
- Obtain samples or an interview from a fibre processor and prospective buyer; distinguish verified overseas pad businesses from unverified Nigerian plantain POP/pad/diaper supply chains.
- Measure collection, dry recovery, saleable yield, rejected material, operating costs, and actual paid orders.
- Ask growers about leaf collection and residue retention alongside fruit yield and soil management.
- Film existing food products separately from laboratory prototypes. Label any illustrative prototype clearly.
- Obtain permission for any third-party footage, figures, or product claims; no source figures or screenshots were copied into the article.
